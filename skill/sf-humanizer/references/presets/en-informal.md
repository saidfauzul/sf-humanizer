## Register purpose

This register covers personal posts, team newsletter items, social posts and notes to friends or close colleagues. The reader knows the writer or is happy to hear a person talking, so voice matters as much as information.

## Target features

- Address and pronouns: "I" and "we" throughout, "you" for the reader, first names as the source uses them. Contractions are the default.
- Sentence length: the `mean_sentence_length` range in the metrics block is only a rough center. Rhythm should be uneven and follow how the writer would say it, and fragments are fine.
- Paragraphs: short and uneven. A one-line paragraph is normal.
- Terms: glossary terms stay exact, even in a joke. Everyday words otherwise, and no explanation of what friends already know.
- Punctuation: avoid em dashes. Use commas, full stops and the odd parenthesis. Exclamation marks stay where the writer used them. Bullets are rare.
- Openings and closings: "Hey," and sign-offs such as "Talk soon" stay when they suit the relationship. Start in the middle of the story or with the news.

## Pattern adjustments

| Pattern id | Treatment | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| EN-04 | softer | Friendly lines such as "Hope your week's going a little smoother than mine." stay. Stock openers still go. |
| EN-06 | stricter | Any "Moreover" or "Furthermore" is out of place here, even a single one. "Anyway" and "Also" are fine. |
| EN-08 | softer | Repetition for a joke or for emphasis is part of the voice. |
| EN-10 | stricter | Personal writing runs as talk. Bullets need a real list behind them. |
| EN-11 | stricter | Nominalizations are rare in this register, so any chain stands out. |
| EN-12 | stricter | "Made a report of it" is never how a person tells a story. |
| EN-14 | softer | "I think" and "about" often stack in speech and show real uncertainty. |
| EN-15 | softer | "Really", "pretty" and "literally" are conversational. "Truly" and "highly" still go. |
| EN-16 | stricter | Even rhythm is conspicuous here and can be acted on alone. |

## Mini examples

- "A bit annoying, but I made a report of it through the catalog. Moreover, the vendor's already replaced it." → "A bit annoying, but I reported it through the catalog and the vendor's already replaced it."
- "Why lunch on Wednesday in the cafeteria? Because it'd be nice to sit down, eat something, relax a bit, and talk about literally anything other than specs and revisions." → "Lunch on Wednesday in the cafeteria? It'd be nice to sit down, eat something, relax a bit, and talk about literally anything other than specs and revisions."
- "Priya joined us on 2 November 2026, and we're truly very happy to have her on board." → "Priya joined us on 2 November 2026, and we're happy to have her on board."

## Limits

- Do not add jokes, asides, anecdotes or slang the source does not have.
- Do not cut a personal detail because it seems off topic.
- Do not make a text more casual than the writer's own relationship with the reader.
- Numbers, dates, names and locked terms stay exact however casual the tone.
