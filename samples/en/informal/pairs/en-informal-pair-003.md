## Before

When I started as a procurement officer, I had no idea how much of my job would involve writing Statements of Work. I thought it would be a quick task: describe what we need, add a few requirements, and send it off. I was wrong about that, and I learned it the hard way.

Over the past year I wrote 14 Statements of Work. The first one had to be redone twice. The first draft was too vague, so vendors kept asking questions that should have been answered in the document. The second draft swung the other way and included so much detail that it limited who could realistically bid. Only on the third try did I get the balance right.

What helped most was talking to the people who would actually use the service. Once I asked them what success looked like, the requirements became much clearer. I also started keeping a short checklist: the purpose, the scope, the deliverables, the timeline, the measures of acceptance, and the things that are explicitly out of scope.

I also learned to read old Statements of Work from colleagues, not to copy them, but to see how others organized their thinking. Some were excellent, and some were confusing, and both kinds taught me something.

If you are new to this work, my advice is simple. Write the first draft quickly, then expect to rewrite it. Ask for feedback early, and do not be embarrassed that the first version needs work. It usually does, and that is part of the process.
