## Before

In today's evolving governance landscape, Delegation of Authority allows an officer to exercise decision-making powers formally assigned to them by a more senior authority. In procurement, this allows approvals to be made at different levels within the organization, provided each delegate has the authority to act and follows the rules that apply to that authority.

It should be noted that delegation does not transfer responsibility. Moreover, a delegate remains accountable for every decision made under the delegated authority, and must be able to show that the decision was properly made. Furthermore, staff should therefore understand the limits of any delegation they hold before exercising it.

One of the most important limits concerns Conflicts of Interest. Section 12(3) of the Sample Housing Agency Procurement Regulation 2026 states: "A delegate shall not approve a purchase in which the delegate has a Conflict of Interest." A delegation therefore cannot be used to approve a purchase in which the delegate has a personal, financial, or other interest that may compromise their judgment.

If you believe you may have a Conflict of Interest in a purchase you are asked to approve, you should make a declaration of it promptly and refrain from approving it. The matter should then be referred to an officer who holds the appropriate authority and has no interest in the purchase.

The carrying out of a regular review of delegations should take place to confirm they remain current. Questions about the scope of a delegation should be raised with the relevant approving officer or with the procurement office before any decision is made.
