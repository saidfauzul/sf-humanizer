## Before

What a Performance Bond Does

In today's fast-paced business world, contracts matter. In many contracts, particularly those for construction, goods supply, and large services, one party needs assurance that the other will carry out the work as agreed. A Performance Bond is one of the most common tools for this. It is a robust financial guarantee, usually issued by a bank or an insurance company, that protects the buyer if the supplier fails to meet its contractual obligations.

It is worth noting that a Performance Bond plays a pivotal role in the reduction of the risk the buyer faces. If the supplier does not deliver, delivers late, or delivers work that does not meet the agreed standard, the buyer may be able to make a claim for compensation under the bond. The buyer then has a source of funds to cover the cost of finding another supplier or correcting the problem, without having to pursue a lengthy dispute.

The bond usually sits alongside the contract rather than replacing it. The contract still sets out the obligations, deadlines, and remedies, and the bond provides the financial backing that makes those obligations meaningful. The amount of the bond is typically expressed as a percentage of the contract value. Moreover, it is often valid for the duration of the contract, sometimes with an additional period for defects or warranty claims.

If you are a supplier, providing a Performance Bond is not only a sign of commitment but also a sign of financial credibility. It can carry a cost, though, and you must arrange it with a guarantor who is willing to take on the risk.

In conclusion, a Performance Bond gives the buyer confidence that a contract will be completed, and it encourages suppliers to honor their commitments from start to finish.
